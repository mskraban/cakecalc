How it works

  1. Area, not diameter. A 9″ round holds about 27% more than an 8″ round, not 12%. Cakecalc compares real pan areas, including rectangles, loaves and bundts.
  2. Everything scales together. Flour, sugar, butter, leavening and spices all use the same factor, so the cake keeps its texture.
  3. Eggs are rounded, then fixed. You get the nearest whole egg and how much beaten egg to add or remove.
  4. Oven time follows depth. Same depth means about the same time. Deeper batter bakes longer at a slightly lower temperature.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes, with no ads, accounts or tracking. The page works offline once loaded; your recipe never leaves your device.

Does it work with metric and European recipes?

Yes. Switch “Show amounts in” to Metric and cups, spoons, ounces and sticks become grams and millilitres, using typical weights for flour, sugar, butter and more. It also reads dl, cl, dag, EL/TL and Slovene units such as žlica and žlička, and has 22, 24, 26 and 28 cm springform and baking-tray sizes.

Can I just change the number of servings?

Yes: choose “Number of servings”, enter what the recipe serves and what you need. Every ingredient is multiplied by the same factor. Keep the same pan size and bake as written, using more pans or batches.

Can I paste a recipe from any website?

Yes. Fractions such as 1/2, 1 1/2 and ½, ranges such as 2–3, and units in cups, spoons, grams, ounces and millilitres are understood. Lines without a quantity (like “salt to taste”) are left alone.

Why does a 9×13″ pan need about 1.2× an 8″ round layer pair?

It comes down to area: 2 × 8″ rounds is 628 cm², and a 9×13″ is 759 cm², so the factor is 1.21. Cakecalc shows this for any pair of pans.

What about cupcakes and bundt pans?

Choose a muffin cup or bundt pan: they are compared by capacity. For cupcakes, pick the muffin size and the number of cups you want to fill.

How accurate is the baking-time advice?

It is a rule of thumb, not a guarantee: oven, pan material and batter all matter. Use it as a starting point and check with a skewer.
